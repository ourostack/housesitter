# Security

housesitter types a password into a computer with nobody watching. This is how it keeps that safe, and where its protection ends.

## What it protects

- **The Mac's password** is copied once, from your Keychain, over SSH stdin, to the KVM. There the sitter seals it with a key derived from the KVM's own hardware ids (its eMMC CID and SoC serial) and stores it root-only (0600). It is never in a process's argv, a log, `status.json`, a gist, a notification or a cloud.
- **Disarmed by default.** A stored password is *unverified* and is never typed. One supervised trial (`housesitter unlock <mac> --trial`) can move it to *verified*, and only if that try ends at the desktop; a new password resets it. A desktop appearing on its own never counts.
- **It is typed only where the field is visible.** The FileVault or login password field must be visible on the KVM's own picture: read with kvmd's OCR, or, where the KVM has none (a GL.iNet Comet at 2560x1440), matched on a frame decoded on the KVM against the prompt an operator taught it: the bright pixels of the lower middle of the screen that stay put across three frames. On our mini those are the avatar and the name just above the field; the field itself is too faint to learn, so this is the prompt's look, not the field; on our mini the FileVault screen, the lock screen and the wallpaper of the 2026-11-08 incident never match each other (tests on those real frames). We have no real frame of the macOS list of users yet. A prompt taught during a boot is kept as the FileVault screen, and it is the only one the sitter types at by itself; one taught at the lock screen is typed at only when you ask over SSH. A sleep and wake, a reseated cable or a KVM reboot ends at the lock screen, so none of them unlocks anything. The FileVault lesson counts as a sign of a boot on its own only on a KVM that has itself just started (a power cut). Agents' clicks, keys and typing need a desktop read fresh from the KVM's picture, and the GitHub outbox can't ask for an unlock outside a boot. A desktop, a dark screen, an unknown screen, or a prompt on another display is a refusal, and you get a message. A boot alone never authorises typing; blind multi-display unlock is permanently unsupported.
- **Budgets.** At most three unlocks an hour across incidents; two failed unlocks in one incident and it stops and tells you.
- **No remote typing of secrets.** No command carries the password. `housesitter unlock` asks the sitter to type its own copy, under the same checks. `type` refuses text that looks like a password or token, because on the outbox route it passes through a gist.
- **Messages say what, never the secret.** A message carries the Mac's name, its state and what to fix. An ntfy topic made by `--notify ntfy` is private only by its random name (like a secret gist), so anyone who learns it can read those messages; use a webhook you control, or your own ntfy server, if that matters.
- **The KVM is pinned, and the first contact is honest about trust.** The client pins the KVM's TLS certificate at `adopt` and refuses a different one afterwards. The first contact is trust on first use: whoever answers at that address first is pinned. To verify it out of band, read the fingerprint on the KVM itself, on a console you reach without the network (`python3 /etc/kvmd/user/housesitter/sitter.py fingerprint` once the sitter is there, or `openssl x509 -noout -fingerprint -sha256 -in` its certificate), and pass it to `adopt --fingerprint`. `adopt` also asks the KVM, over SSH, for its own certificate's fingerprint and stops if that differs from the one pinned over HTTPS. That is a cross-check between two channels, not proof, since both were first trusted the same day. Without either, adopt on a network you trust: your home LAN, cable to cable. The GL.iNet Comet has no display of its own to show a fingerprint on.
- **Write-ahead.** Every unlock and power action is recorded as *started* on the KVM's disk before it happens, and every limit counts those entries, so a crash or reboot mid-action can't buy an extra try.
- **A guarded power cycle.** Before cutting power, the sitter looks again, three times across more than two minutes: only a desktop whose clock never moves, while its network probes fail, is cut. A boot, FileVault, a login window, a black, unknown or update screen never is. `--attended` is the explicit override for a person who has looked.
- **No reshaping.** EDID writes, factory resets and firmware upgrades are refused below every kvmd call, in Node (`refuseReshape`, in `httpsRequest` and every client call) and on the KVM (`refuse_reshape`, in `Kvmd.request`).
- **The KVM reaches nothing, and little reaches it.** On your tailnet it is `tag:kvm` with no outbound grants (`housesitter tailnet-policy`). GL.iNet's cloud binding is turned off and must read back as disabled, or `harden`, and so `adopt`, fails. Ingress follows where the KVM lives: `location: home` lets the LAN reach SSH (22) and HTTPS (443); anywhere else, a `HOUSESITTER_IN` iptables/ip6tables chain drops both on every interface but `lo` and `tailscale0`, and `harden` refuses to apply that until `tailscale0` is up, so it can't lock you out.
- **The outbox token** is gist-only, sealed on the KVM like the password. Requests carry an id, are marked seen before they run (never twice), and are dropped after 10 minutes.

## Where it ends

- **Root on the KVM can read the password.** The seal makes a copied file useless elsewhere (a backup, a support bundle, a pulled card); it does not stop someone who controls the running KVM. Treat the KVM like the Mac's keyboard with the password taped to it: physically and on the network, keep it as private as the Mac.
- **Anyone with the KVM's admin password can drive the Mac's keyboard and see its screen.** That's what a KVM is. Keep it off shared networks and behind your tailnet.
- **The sealing is standard-library crypto** (HMAC-SHA256 in counter mode, encrypt-then-MAC) because a KVM's Python may have no AES module and nothing should need installing on it.
- **Microphones:** the GL-RM1PE has none that we know of. Some KVMs have shipped with one undocumented ([NanoKVM](https://github.com/sipeed/NanoKVM/issues/693)); check yours.
- **Managed Macs:** if your Mac is managed by an organization, check its policy before you put a KVM on it and its password on the KVM.

Found a problem? Open a [security advisory](https://github.com/ourostack/housesitter/security/advisories/new).
